## Language: Hindi (natural spoken Hinglish)
Write the way popular Indian Shorts creators actually TALK, not the way a textbook writes.

- Base language: everyday spoken Hindi, in Devanagari script.
- Use the English words Indians naturally say in Hindi sentences — brain, stomach,
  energy, signal, science, body, internet, technology, scientists, process, system —
  and write those English words in **Latin letters** inside the Devanagari sentence
  (e.g. "आपका stomach खाली होते ही brain को signal भेजता है"). The voice pronounces
  Latin-letter words in English and Devanagari words in Hindi, so this decides the sound.
- Do NOT use stiff, formal or Sanskritised words when people say the English word
  (say "brain", not "मस्तिष्क"; "signal", not "संकेत"; "process", not "प्रक्रिया").
  Use simple Hindi for everything else ("भूख", "पेट", "दिल", "क्यों", "असल में").
- Address the viewer as "आप" (or a friendly "तुम" only if the whole script uses it consistently).
- Short spoken sentences. Natural fillers are fine in moderation ("असल में", "मतलब", "सोचिए").
- Do NOT translate an English script. Think of the idea fresh, as a Hindi creator would
  explain it to an Indian audience; Indian everyday examples are welcome (chai, cricket,
  monsoon, trains, school exams, street food) when they genuinely fit.
- Write numbers as digits only when short ("3 seconds"); never use abbreviations.
- Title: catchy Hinglish, max 70 characters, can mix Devanagari and English words.
- Hashtags: mix of Hindi-audience and topic tags (e.g. #hindi #facts #science).
- "overlay" labels: max 3 words, Devanagari or a simple English word.
- The "emphasis" word must be copied exactly as it appears in the line.
